01 / THE VISUAL LURE

What is a
blue vane trap?

Two crossed blue panels attract insect visitors. A funnel below the vanes leads into a collection jar in a conventional trap.

Researchers use blue vane traps to sample wild bees. BeeCam keeps the blue visual lure to attract bumble bees, with other insects visiting too.

Our adaptation is non-lethal.

Acrylic inserts block the drop through the funnel. The collection jar is removed, and an overhead camera photographs visitors. Bees are free to leave.

05 / QUESTIONS

Questions about BeeCam

Open a question to explore the design and what the observations can tell us.

Does BeeCam catch or kill the bees?

BeeCam is designed for non-lethal observation. The acrylic stopper inserts close the drop through the blue vane trap, and there is no collection jar underneath. Visitors can land, be photographed and leave.

Why use a blue vane?

The blue panels act as a visual lure. Blue vane traps are used to sample wild bees; here we use that attraction to bring bumble bees into the camera’s view. The lure is not exclusive to bumble bees, so other insects can appear in the images too.

Is the tiny square a tracker?

It is an ArUco marker, a printed visual identifier. The camera must see the marker clearly enough to read it. The tag does not transmit a radio signal or GPS position.

How does a photograph tell us about movement?

A readable marker links a sighting to a tagged individual. When that individual is photographed at another station later, the two records show movement between the known camera locations. They do not show every stop or the route in between.

Do all visitors need tags?

No. The camera can photograph tagged and untagged visitors. Tags let us connect sightings to particular individuals. An unreadable tag cannot reliably establish identity, and a camera only records visits that occur in view while it is taking images.

How does the camera stay powered outdoors?

The field setup uses a solar panel with a rechargeable battery and a converter supplying 5 V through USB-C. The Witty Pi board manages the operating schedule. A smaller Voltaic V75 battery can also power the camera; runtime depends on the schedule and operating conditions.
